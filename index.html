Before designing any screen,
I listed what needed to change.

1

Long loading times

Long loading times and slow navigation between pages reduced the flow of reading the news, hurting how long people stayed on the portal.

2

Undervalued content

Events and podcasts were easy to find, but the presentation was simple and didn't highlight the key information or how the content was organized.

3

No clear information hierarchy

Many elements with similar proportions and no clear visual division, plus few accessibility features.

Three decisions that transformed the portal

The main design decisions, the challenges behind them and the results achieved.

Decision 1 · Content sections

Each content format with the presentation it deserves

Problem
Formats beyond news had little prominence: events and podcasts were presented simply, and there was no webstories area.
Decision
I created the Webstories section from scratch and reorganized Events and Podcasts, giving each format its own structure and direct access from the menu.
Effect
The different content formats gained space and prominence within the portal navigation. Their own pages also helped organize the content and get it indexed by search engines.
Decision 2 · Podcast

From channel to episode, with simpler navigation

Problem
Podcasts existed on the portal, but there was no clear separation by channel. That made it hard to browse the content, watch episodes and keep up with the latest ones.
Decision
I separated the podcasts by channel and designed a page for each one, where users watch episodes and see the latest ones, organized by season.
Effect
A more direct journey from discovering channels to choosing episodes and keeping up with the latest releases, all integrated into the portal's visual standards.
Decision 3 · Accessibility

Accessibility built into the experience and the Design System

Problem
The previous portal offered few accessibility features, limiting how the experience could adapt to different users.
Decision
I integrated an accessibility menu into the portal, available on every page, and incorporated accessibility criteria into the Design System components.
Effect
The evaluated pages achieved an average accessibility score of 94 on desktop, above the benchmark of 87 set by the six major news portals analyzed.

Mobile version

I adapted the desktop experience for mobile, with navigation and components designed specifically for mobile devices. The Design System components maintain the same information hierarchy across both formats, ensuring a clear and consistent reading experience on any device.

What I take from this project

Redesigning a portal with more than 80,000 news articles was an exercise in understanding how to organize large volumes of information without compromising the navigation experience. Throughout the project, I saw how important it is to think about information architecture, content hierarchy and how easy it is to find what you are looking for before moving on to visual decisions.

It was also an important experience to understand how decisions about structure, accessibility and organization can shape the experience of a product as a whole.

About the project

Context

Beekeeping still relies on manual and poorly standardized processes. Without reliable data and management tools, beekeepers face low predictability, productivity losses, and difficulty tracking hives, tasks, and teams. This makes management reactive, unsustainable, and vulnerable to unforeseen events.

Problem

Essential data such as temperature, humidity, production, and hive conditions is hard to record and track. Tasks, teams, and apiary visits don't follow a structured workflow, so beekeepers only discover a problem once it has already caused impact. Without indicators, productivity and hive welfare are compromised.

Challenge

Develop a digital solution capable of modernizing beekeeping management by bringing intelligent monitoring, data centralization, and organization to all stages, without adding complexity to the beekeeper's daily routine.

BBEE — Smart Apiculture is a web and mobile platform that brings together apiary management, intelligent monitoring, and operational organization. It centralizes data, tasks, location, and production, so beekeepers can act preventively, strategically, and sustainably.

Fieldwork is the heart of beekeeping management.

  • Teamwork in the apiary, with a clear and organized workflow.

Solution flow

The customer's CPF is entered to access their profile and consult essential information, such as address and contracted plans. This data helps the salesperson understand the customer profile and make a more assertive offer.

Offers available according to the customer's credit score. On the contracting data screen, the customer provides the address that will be linked to the offer to check coverage availability.

After the address is provided, we analyze the available coverage and start configuring the offer. In this step, the lines that will be linked to the chosen plan are defined, including the mobile and landline voice lines the customer wants to use.

If the plan includes fiber, installation must be scheduled, with two date options for the technician's visit to the customer's home. Next, a summary screen is presented, where all information defined with the customer is reviewed before the sale is completed.

As a security step, the customer's identity is verified through biometrics before the sale is finalized. After identity validation, the sale is completed successfully.
